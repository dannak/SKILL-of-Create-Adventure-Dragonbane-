# Opening Scene Checklist

Every opening scene must pass all four gates before scene-expansion work begins.
The skill cites the specific beat that satisfies each gate, or flags the gap and
proposes a fix.

---

## Gate 1 — In Medias Res

**The scene starts in the middle of something happening — not traveling toward it.**

The party must be present at an unfolding situation when the first sentence
is read aloud. There is no lead-up, no tavern briefing, no journey scene
before the action.

Passing examples:
- The cart they're riding lurches as the bridge cracks beneath it.
- They're already inside the burning warehouse when the scene opens.
- The negotiation has just broken down; a dagger is on the table.

Failing examples:
- "You arrive in the village of Thornwick and head to the inn."
- "A stranger approaches you on the road and asks for your help."
- "The council has summoned you to discuss a problem."

---

## Gate 2 — Stated Goal

**The party has a clear, actionable goal within the first two exchanges.**

The goal must be:
- Concrete (something they can physically do or decide)
- Stated or strongly implied by the situation — not deduced from backstory
- Connected to what will become the adventure's climax

The goal does not have to be the "true" goal of the adventure; it can be an
immediate goal that opens onto the larger one. But a party that finishes the
opening scene must know what they are trying to do next.

---

## Gate 3 — Sensory Detail

**At least three distinct senses are engaged in the opening description.**

Sight is default and does not count on its own. The description must include
at least two of: sound, smell, touch/texture/temperature, taste, proprioception
(movement, balance, physical sensation).

Phrasing guide:
- Sound: "the crack of splitting timber," "a child crying two streets over"
- Smell: "iron and mildew," "pine resin and distant smoke"
- Touch: "the deck tilts hard to port," "gravel biting through thin boots"
- Temperature: "a cold that seeps rather than bites"

---

## Gate 4 — Decision Point

**The scene ends (or is designed to end) with a meaningful choice for the party.**

The decision point must:
- Have at least two distinct options with different consequences
- Not have an obvious single correct answer
- Not be "do you proceed or not" (too binary and low-stakes)

Examples of strong decision points:
- Save the burning building's occupants or pursue the arsonist?
- Trust the injured guard's story or detain him until you can verify it?
- Use the one dose of antidote now on the child, or hold it for the negotiator
  you'll need later?

---

## Audit format

When evaluating an opening scene, output exactly this block:

```
OPENING SCENE GATE AUDIT
─────────────────────────
Gate 1 — In Medias Res:   [PASS/FAIL]
  Citation / Fix: ...

Gate 2 — Stated Goal:     [PASS/FAIL]
  Citation / Fix: ...

Gate 3 — Sensory Detail:  [PASS/FAIL]
  Senses present: ...
  Citation / Fix: ...

Gate 4 — Decision Point:  [PASS/FAIL]
  Options: ...
  Citation / Fix: ...

GATE STATUS: GREEN / RED
```

Do not advance past Step 5 while GATE STATUS is RED.
