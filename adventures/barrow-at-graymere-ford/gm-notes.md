# GM Notes — The Barrow at Graymere Ford

---

## Pacing Notes

This is a short adventure with a clear three-beat structure: arrival/combat, investigation, dungeon/climax. The middle scene (Scene 2) is the pacing valve — it can run 10 minutes if the party skips straight to the barrow, or 30 minutes if they dig into Agna's secret and explore Torben's cottage.

The wight encounter should feel like a genuine threat. Don't pull its punches on Dread Aura or Touch of the Grave in round 1 — the heroic payoff comes from surviving a hard fight or finding the clever solution, not from the threat being soft.

The sealing-the-urn option exists specifically to reward players who talked to people before entering the barrow. If they went in without talking to Agna or visiting Torben's cottage, the option still exists but they'll have to figure out the silver requirement from the wight's own dialogue.

**Scene pacing targets:**

| Scene | Estimated duration | Pacing note |
|-------|--------------------|-------------|
| 1 — The Crossing | 15–20 min | Fast; combat, then relief. Don't linger on Liss/Marta until Scene 2. |

## Fail-Forward Paths

**Scene 1 — The Crossing:**
If the party is defeated: village men drive the Risen off with fire. Party wakes on the near bank. Marta is unharmed; the Risen retreated, not satisfied. The adventure continues; the party is down some HP and has lost the initiative.

**Scene 2 — The Elder's Story:**
If the party skips Agna entirely: they enter the barrow without knowing about Torben, the urn, or the pact mechanics. The wight's dialogue in Scene 4 can fill in the gaps — it knows all of this. The sealing option is harder to find but still available.

If the party fails to uncover Agna's secret: she doesn't volunteer it, but it doesn't block anything. The adventure resolves; she lives with her guilt. Good future-session material.

**Scene 3 — Outer Chambers:**
If the party falls in the pit: they take damage and climb out. The wight doesn't pursue into the pit.

If the party fails to free Torben: they can restrain him, tie him up (he forgives this immediately after), or simply push past and leave him in the passage. He recovers after the climax.

**Scene 4 — The Wight's Hall:**
If the party is defeated: wight takes one valued item per character as the debt paid. Party wakes outside at dawn. Marta's pull fades. Barrow is dormant but the pact is still broken — future sessions can revisit this.

If the party tries to talk to the wight with no silver and no knowledge of the resealing process: the wight tells them, in its flat declarative way, exactly what is required — silver, the marks, two pairs of hands. It wants the pact restored. It's not trying to be obscure.

## Contingency Plans

**If the party kills Torben in Scene 3:**
The wight's dialogue in Scene 4 covers the resealing process if asked. The party can improvise silver from their own gear (coins, jewelry). The victory condition still works; the aftermath is just sadder. Agna will grieve; this is worth playing.

**If the party refuses to enter the barrow:**
The wight's hold on Marta grows. Each night she sleepwalks closer. By the third night she walks into the barrow on her own. This gives the party one more session to act before a child is taken. Agna ages ten years in front of them. Strong motivation to act.

**If the party tries to permanently destroy the barrow (collapse the entrance, etc.):**
Structurally possible — but the wight walks through stone. Collapsing the entry just means it has to dig out, which takes a few days. The village gets a brief reprieve, then the problem comes back worse (the wight is now also angry). Collapsing the entry with the wight inside and the urn already sealed works — the pact is restored and the wight has no reason to emerge.

**If the party sides with the wight:**
The wight wants the debt paid, not allies. But if the party offers to bring Agna to the mound to pay the debt personally (delivering the person responsible), the wight will accept this as a valid interpretation of the pact terms. It takes Agna's confession and her presence at the mound as payment — doesn't harm her, just makes her watch as it reseals the urn with her own hands. Agna accepts. She says it's fair.
